What we do

Strengthening the recycling sector

ASWRA works to organise, support and advance waste recyclers and recycling enterprises across Anambra State.

Our activities

1

Sector organisation

Bringing eligible recyclers, recovery workers, aggregators, enterprises and cooperatives into a shared membership platform.

2

Capacity development

Promoting training, workshops, technical knowledge and safer, more responsible working practices for members.

3

Industry coordination

Encouraging cooperation across the recovery and recycling value chain and sharing relevant market and industry information.

4

Advocacy and sustainability

Facilitating constructive engagement on members’ concerns and promoting responsible resource recovery and environmental protection.

How ASWRA facilitates opportunities

Subject to its approved rules, available programmes, partner arrangements and eligibility criteria, ASWRA may facilitate or share information about opportunities such as:

Market and off taker linkages

Facilitating connections with recognised buyers and approved aggregation arrangements. Prices and terms are agreed transparently and may vary with the market.

Producer funded recycling programmes

Facilitating access to applicable extended producer responsibility (EPR) initiatives for eligible materials such as PET plastics and used beverage cans, where available and subject to programme rules.

Health insurance information

Information and help with applications to relevant health insurance programmes, including the Anambra State Health Insurance Agency (ASHIA), subject to that agency's eligibility and payment requirements.

Cooperative and thrift opportunities

Access to approved savings and credit initiatives through a duly established, authorised cooperative with its own constitution and financial rules.

Please note. Membership does not guarantee loans, grants, contracts, government appointments, producer payments or admission into any third party programme.

Across the value chain

Practical support for different roles in recycling

The needs of a collector are not always the same as those of an aggregator, processor or recycling enterprise. ASWRA's sector-development approach recognises these differences while encouraging cooperation between participants.

For collectors and recovery workers

Promote access to relevant information, safe handling practices, opportunities for skills development and a stronger collective voice on issues affecting day-to-day work.

For aggregators and cooperatives

Encourage reliable coordination, material quality awareness, transparent business relationships and access to relevant market information and legitimate buyers.

For recycling enterprises

Support constructive connections across the supply chain, exchange of industry information and engagement on practical challenges affecting responsible business growth.

Responsible practice matters

A stronger recycling sector must also be a responsible one. Growth should not come at the expense of worker safety, community wellbeing or the environment.

  • Encourage safe handling, storage and transportation of recovered materials.
  • Promote fair and transparent weighing, grading and trading practices.
  • Discourage child labour, exploitation, theft and unlawful sourcing of materials.
  • Reduce avoidable contamination and prevent recovered materials from creating new pollution.
  • Encourage lawful operations and respect for relevant environmental and public health requirements.

What member support can look like in practice

Association activities are intended to translate shared goals into practical engagement. Depending on resources and programme arrangements, support may take forms such as:

Information and coordination

Sharing relevant sector updates, organising communication among members and helping participants identify common challenges that require collective attention.

Learning and safer practice

Encouraging access to practical knowledge on sorting, handling, storage, contamination prevention, personal safety and responsible work methods.

Business readiness

Promoting good records, transparent weighing, material grading, consistent supply practices and professional relationships that can help operators work more effectively.

Collective engagement

Creating channels for members to communicate concerns and participate in discussions with relevant stakeholders when such engagements are arranged.

How members can participate

  • Keep contact and membership information up to date.
  • Share relevant challenges and ideas respectfully.
  • Take part in confirmed meetings, trainings or programmes.
  • Observe applicable laws, safety guidance and environmental requirements.
  • Provide accurate information and avoid presenting unconfirmed opportunities as guaranteed benefits.

Activities are announced when details are confirmed. Membership does not automatically guarantee access to funding, equipment, contracts, insurance or market opportunities.
