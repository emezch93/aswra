Governance and accountability

ASWRA’s leadership is expected to act in the Association’s interests, follow its constitution and approved policies, communicate responsibly and remain accountable to its members. The principles below provide a framework for responsible service.

  • Loyalty
  • Integrity
  • Respect
  • Commitment
  • Professionalism
  • Disclosure of conflicts of interest
  • Confidentiality
  • Discipline
  • Service
  • Sustainability

Responsible service

What effective coordination should provide

Leading a membership association involves more than representing its name. It requires listening to members, communicating decisions clearly, coordinating agreed activities and ensuring that the Association's work remains aligned with its constitution and approved policies.

Clear coordination

Helping members and stakeholders understand the Association's priorities, current activities and appropriate channels for enquiries or proposals.

Member representation

Creating constructive ways for different parts of the recycling value chain to raise concerns and contribute to discussions affecting the sector.

Accountable administration

Maintaining reliable records, respecting approved procedures and communicating the status of decisions and initiatives accurately.

Member participation strengthens governance

An association is stronger when its members understand its objectives, participate constructively and hold its leadership to the standards established by its governing documents.

  • Members should use recognised channels to raise questions and submit proposals.
  • Leadership should communicate confirmed decisions and avoid promising unapproved benefits.
  • Conflicts of interest should be disclosed and managed responsibly.
  • Personal and application information should be handled confidentially.
  • Public statements should distinguish confirmed activities from proposals and future plans.

Leadership, coordination and member accountability

Effective association leadership requires more than representing the organisation publicly. It also means creating clear communication, supporting member participation, maintaining reliable records and treating decisions responsibly.

Direction and representation

Leadership communicates the Association’s objectives, represents its interests in appropriate engagements and helps keep activities aligned with its stated purpose.

Coordination and communication

Coordination supports information sharing, member contact, programme organisation and follow-through on agreed actions.

Transparency and stewardship

Responsible administration encourages clear records, careful handling of Association resources, accurate reporting and appropriate accountability.

Member voice

A credible association listens to members, makes room for constructive feedback and encourages participation from different parts of the recycling value chain.

These are general principles for effective association governance. They do not replace ASWRA’s constitution, formal decisions or any rules governing the Association. Official roles and appointments should be confirmed through the Association’s recognised records.
